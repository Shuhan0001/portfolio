SUPERMARKET
TOGETHER

A low-poly management sim about stocking shelves, pricing goods, and turning one small shop into a full supermarket.

"Buy low, price it right, keep the shelves full — and lay out a floor plan customers actually enjoy walking through."

Unity Management Sim Low-Poly Collaborative Team

00 / Overview

Supermarket Together blends RPG-style progression with sim-building mechanics: players lay out their own store, stock and price the goods, hire staff, and reinvest the profit into bigger spaces and pricier inventory. The demo's target is a small but fully playable slice — a handful of buildable structures, a couple of controllable characters, a first batch of sellable goods, and a short story hook.

01 / Core Loop

01
Order stock — restock inventory while wholesale prices are low
02
Restock shelves — carry boxes from the loading dock onto the shop floor
03
Set prices — stay within the range customers are willing to pay
04
Open the store — customers browse the floor and pick up items
05
Checkout — scan each item, take payment, and make change

02 / Systems

Staff AI

Recruited NPCs each take on a single assigned role. Efficiency differs from character to character, and mistreating them lowers morale and productivity.

Dynamic Pricing

Wholesale prices fluctuate like a market, rewarding players who buy in when costs dip. Each customer has their own acceptable price range — go beyond it and demand drops off sharply.

Store Building

Modular zones such as the bakery and produce section can be paired for bonuses, or clash and hurt sales — a restroom next to the deli counter, for example.

Story Hooks

Reaching a revenue milestone can trigger a conversation with a randomly chosen NPC, who offers a quest that's demanding to complete but pays off well.

03 / My Role

Game design and interaction design on a four-person team, working alongside a narrative designer and an artist covering both character and environment work.

04 / Status

Currently building the first playable loop — order, restock, price, open, checkout — using two starter goods to test pricing, shelving and the basic building tools before the demo's scope grows.
