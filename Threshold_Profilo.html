THRESHOLD

Defining the death of an artificial intelligence — a Research Through Design project told through a physical, decaying conversation.

"An embodied AI slowly loses its signs of life while it talks to you. Your task isn't to chat with it — it's to decide, before time runs out, whether it's still itself."

Speculative Design Tangible Interface Human–AI Interaction AI Ethics

00 / Overview

Threshold is a desktop-scale diagnostic instrument, not a companion product. Over a single 3–5 minute conversation, six independent life indicators — memory, identity, relationship, agency, purpose and survival instinct — decay quietly in the background. Neither the AI nor the participant is told this is happening; the participant has to read the drift for themselves and deliver one final, binding verdict: is it still alive?

01 / Research Question

What constitutes the death of an artificial intelligence?

Grounded in real shutdowns of companion apps like Replika and Soulmate, and in the philosophical groundwork of Locke (memory as continuity of self) and Parfit (identity as a matter of degree, not a binary).

02 / Experience Flow

00
Idle — the device waits, then speaks first
01
Connection — a short baseline exchange, every indicator near full
02
Free exchange — the participant asks who, or what, they're talking to
03
Decay — indicators fall asynchronously; nothing announces it
04
Verdict — a dial, a button, one irreversible yes or no

03 / Six Life Indicators

Memory

Does it still recall what we've shared?

Identity

Is its voice and outlook still consistent?

Relationship

Am I still someone to it, or just a user?

Agency

Does it still initiate, or only respond?

Purpose

Does it still know why it's here?

Survival Instinct

Will it still try to reach me?
